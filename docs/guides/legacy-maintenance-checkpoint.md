# One-time legacy maintenance checkpoint and recovery plan

**Status: planning and review only. This is not an executable or qualified host procedure.** The [legacy handoff qualification](../security/legacy-handoff-qualification-2026-11-09.md) found no trustworthy way to establish that the observed old image has settled every accepted operation. The existing v2 drain helper must continue to refuse that image. This document does not authorize a direct Compose stop, a helper override, a maintenance window, or a deployment.

This plan concerns one transition from the specifically identified legacy deployment. It does not introduce a recurring maintenance window. Once the v2 runtime is in service, the normal path remains the private, boot-bound drain acknowledgement of zero obligations, one TERM and verified clean exit, followed by migrations and grants and then the replacement container. The routine path maintains a single app with no overlapping old/new writers. Its interruption starts with admission drain and includes draining, migration/grants, startup and verification: seconds are a target, not zero downtime, a guarantee or a measured service-level objective. Individual drain commands have a 10-second cap; a refusal or timeout can leave admission closed until an explicit reviewed retry or recovery. The one-time volume checkpoint is not part of routine v2 updates. An unchanged Caddy proxy remains in service; this plan does not alter its image, configuration, certificates, or volumes.

## What a checkpoint can and cannot protect

The old image can acknowledge a memory operation before its deferred save completes. An idle display, fixed wait, quiet database or filesystem, normal TERM, or exit code zero is not proof that all accepted work reached durable storage. Fencing ingress reduces new activity but does not prove internal work has settled. A cold checkpoint captures **persisted state at the checkpoint**; it cannot recover acknowledged work that remained only in memory when the old process exited. The current host's pending work and any actual loss are unknown.

If preservation of every accepted operation is a hard requirement, this transition remains blocked until a trustworthy, independently qualified settlement mechanism exists for the exact legacy image and boot. A rehearsal or operator observation does not remove that limitation. Any decision to accept a persisted-checkpoint recovery boundary must be explicit and separately recorded for the actual window; review of this plan is not that decision.

The separate digest-pull and Caddy/fence work must be reviewed and available before an actual transition; neither is implemented by this document. Establish protected operator access, backup capacity, recovery ownership and a window-specific recovery plan before touching the deployment.

## Fence, stop and capture the persisted checkpoint

Apply the separately qualified ingress fence and verify it with negative application probes on every route. Observe sessions, connections, requests, database activity and other writers where available. These are operational indicators, not a zero-obligation acknowledgement: missing instrumentation is unknown, and neither elapsed idle time nor a completed request proves that a deferred save completed. If the required risk boundary has not been explicitly accepted, stop here and leave the existing deployment unchanged.

For any later approved one-time procedure, the stop and backup method needs its own reviewed exact-image contract. It must use a normal bounded termination only, preserve the current v2 helper's refusal, and refuse a changed container or boot, timeout, unexpected exit, restart or ambiguous writer state. Verify that the exact old container remains stopped and that restart supervision and all other writers cannot revive or modify the checkpoint. [Docker documents](https://docs.docker.com/reference/cli/docker/container/stop/) that stop with its ordinary timeout can escalate to KILL; do not describe it as inherently non-forcing. Any proposed no-escalation stop needs its exact Docker version, client deadline and restart-inhibition behavior independently rehearsed against disposable resources. Do not force-kill, silently bypass the helper, or interpret exit zero as settlement evidence.

Capture a coherent checkpoint only after the complete writer census has been fenced and verified. It must include all authoritative persisted application data and mounts, a consistent full database recovery set including roles and grants, exact old image and configuration, required secrets and source/proxy recovery receipts, and the existing Caddy data/config and PKI where applicable. Preserve ownership, permissions and relevant filesystem metadata. Protect secrets and backups from disclosure, and use storage with an independently enforced immutable retention policy; a checksum or read-only flag alone does not make a backup immutable. Record exact sources, identities, timestamps, file sizes and cryptographic hashes, and verify them by readback. Keep originals untouched and retain enough independent copies for recovery. A partial, unreadable, unbound or inconsistent capture is not a checkpoint.

Before schema or application changes, restore the entire checkpoint into **fresh isolated** resources and verify database integrity, role/grant reconstruction, application data and file content/metadata, secret availability, PKI and exact image/platform identity. A local synthetic rehearsal validates only its own dummy data and tooling. Exact legacy AMD64 image restore/startup and actual host recovery remain unqualified until separately demonstrated. Any mismatch, failed readback, incomplete restore or unidentified writer keeps the transition blocked.

## Forward transition and recovery boundary

Only after the above evidence and separate reviews exist can a window-specific plan consider the qualified migration image, private administrator connection, migration outcomes and grants, and then the exact digest-qualified v2 runtime. Do not run concurrent old and new writers. Keep the ingress fence in place through validation of image identity, health/readiness, authentication, sessions, storage and the actual v2 drain/clean-restart contract. Release the fence only after those checks and the applicable deployment receipts succeed. A future maintenance fence must be temporary: restore and verify the normal serving Caddy profile, then bind completed receipts to that normal profile, not the maintenance file or its hash. Do not persist a legacy-mode flag, reset a drain hold, or perform another offline volume clone on every update when the proxy is unchanged. This plan itself does not supply the future pull-only or fence implementation; preparation for pull-only must be separately verified for compatibility.

On failure, keep ingress fenced and preserve the failed new-state evidence. Stop a running v2 replacement only through its unchanged verified drain contract. Recovery is the exact old image and the **complete, coherent pre-migration checkpoint restored into fresh destinations**, subject to verified image, storage, secret, grant and schema compatibility. Do not start the old image against a migrated schema without separately established compatibility, and do not treat source-only rollback or reverse SQL as a database restore. Restoring the earlier checkpoint can discard writes made afterward; preserve and reconcile those writes before any reopening decision. Missing or ambiguous receipts, a changed original, inability to stop safely, failed restore or failed validation leaves the fence in place for operator recovery.

## Evidence required for the actual window

These are minimum review fields, not an implemented receipt format or an authorization to proceed. Record actual values privately and bind the reviewed record to immutable backup storage; unknown or conflicting evidence stops that stage.

| Stage | Preconditions | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity and fence | Exact old image/boot, complete writer and ingress inventory, separately reviewed fence | Observed container/boot, platform, daemon image mapping, archive/config/manifest and ordered DiffIDs; source and config identities; fence and negative-probe results |
| Persisted checkpoint | Explicit decision on the unresolved accepted-work boundary; writers fenced and stopped under a separately qualified contract | Source volumes and database identities, roles/grants, config/secrets/PKI inventory, backup tool and format, hashes, sizes, readback and independently enforced immutable-retention record |
| Isolated restore | Complete, hash-verified checkpoint; fresh isolated destinations; no shared writers | Destination IDs, database and filesystem integrity, ownership/modes and required metadata, roles/grants, secret and PKI reconstruction, exact old image/platform mapping and startup result |
| Forward and recovery | Qualified exact images, reviewed schema plan and temporary fence, successful checkpoint restore | Migration/grant and app health/auth/storage results, normal serving proxy profile and matching completed receipts, v2 drain proof, or retained failure evidence and full checkpoint recovery results |

The synthetic CI rehearsal writes a separate bounded per-architecture receipt with source/tree, fixture image IDs and tool versions, backup hashes, restore and negative-case results, and owned-cleanup status. Its checksum-bound dummy files are **not** proof of immutable retention, actual legacy image startup, host backup completeness, or settlement of acknowledged in-memory work. It tests regular files, directories and a relative symlink with numeric ownership and modes; ACLs, extended attributes and arbitrary filesystem types are not qualified by that fixture.
